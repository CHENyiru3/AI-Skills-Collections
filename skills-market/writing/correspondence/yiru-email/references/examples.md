# Worked examples

The names and scenarios below are fictional. Use the draft and the recipient’s relationship with Yiru to choose the wording.

## Light edit to an existing reply

Yiru asks for a grammar fix in this draft.

> Hi Alex,
>
> Thank you for sharing the slides! I’m glad to learn more about the project and looking forward to discuss it with you.
>
> Best,\
> Yiru

Return the edited reply.

> Hi Alex,
>
> Thank you for sharing the slides! I’m glad to learn more about the project and look forward to discussing it with you.
>
> Best,\
> Yiru

The edit repairs the verb forms. Yiru’s thanks, enthusiasm, and sign-off already work.

## Ask a new administrator for access

Yiru’s request to use a shared workspace was approved yesterday. The invitation has yet to arrive. The office receiving this first email did not handle the approval.

**Subject** — Access to the shared workspace

> Dear Support Team,
>
> My request to use the shared workspace was approved yesterday. I don’t think I’ve received an invitation yet. Could you let me know how to activate my account?
>
> Best regards,\
> Yiru Chen

The approval explains why Yiru is asking about account access and gives the office a place to start.

## Follow up after a research discussion

Yiru’s notes say the professor suggested checking whether the two batches had similar cell-type proportions. The conversation prompted Yiru to reconsider the comparison. Yiru wants to ask which annotation to use, and the thread identifies two options.

> Dear Prof. Lee,
>
> Thank you for taking the time to discuss the batch comparison with me. Your question about cell-type proportions made me rethink how I was interpreting the difference between the batches.
>
> For that check, would you suggest using the original cell-type labels or the harmonized annotations?
>
> Best regards,\
> Yiru

The email names the suggestion that mattered and asks a question the professor can answer directly.
